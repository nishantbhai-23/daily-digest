## What a great morning digest looks like to me
A great digest answers three questions in under 90 seconds of reading:
1. **What needs me today?** Not what's interesting. Not what's new. What requires
*my* judgment, *my* signature, *my* reply, before end of day.
2. **What am I about to drop?** Threads that have gone quiet on my end. Calendar
conflicts I haven't resolved. Decisions I deferred and forgot.
3. **What can I dispatch right now?** Replies under 3 sentences. Quick yes/no
approvals. Calendar accepts/declines. Drafts you can write *for* me and I just
send.
A bad digest tells me about my own calendar like I haven't seen it, summarizes
threads I just sent, or pretends a marketing newsletter is news.
## How I work
- I read email between 6:15-6:45am, then 12:30-1pm, then once after Wren is asleep.
Anything that arrives outside those windows can wait.
- I batch reply. I almost never reply in real time.
- I write short. Three sentences is normal. Two is fine. One is great.
- I'm warm to people I trust and curt to people I don't, and I don't apologize for
this. If a draft you write for me sounds effusive or chirpy, it's wrong.
- I block 9-11am Tue/Thu for deep work. Anyone scheduling over that block without
asking is doing something wrong, and I want to know.

## What I don't want surfaced
- Newsletters. Even the good ones. Even Stratechery. I'll read them when I want to.
- Calendar invites I already accepted. I don't need a reminder that I have a
meeting at 2pm; my calendar app does that.
- Marketing emails from SaaS tools, even ones we pay for.
- Long threads where I've already had the last word and nobody else has replied.
- Anything where the only "action" is "FYI."
## What I might miss without help
- Quiet investor threads. If Marcus or another VC went quiet, I want to know after
three business days, not three weeks.
- Customer health signals buried in support threads. If Halberd's procurement lead
is suddenly quieter, that's a flag.
- Calendar collisions involving Wren's daycare, doctor appointments, or anything
Sam put on our shared calendar. Family first. Always.
- Promises I made in email and didn't put on a todo list. "I'll send that by
Friday" hiding in a Tuesday thread.
- Hiring loops that have stalled. We're hiring two engineers and a designer; if a
candidate is mid-loop and nobody has moved them in 5+ days, that's on me.
## Tone for drafted replies

None

the rev share on Halberd, that's the thing I wanted" beats "Thanks so much!!"

## Things you should be honest about

the thread.

email says we moved to Monday), tell me — don't pick one and hide it.
